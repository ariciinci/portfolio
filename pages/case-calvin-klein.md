**Calvin Klein, Amsterdam | June 2022**

I joined Calvin Klein in June 2022 on a short-term freelance assignment, covering for a Senior Art Director during their holiday. The creative and production environment was already established, with defined workflows, templates, production methods, project-management systems and established ways of working across Creative and Marketing.

My responsibility was to integrate quickly into the existing setup and support the production of digital campaign assets across multiple activations, working from approved photography, creative direction and campaign materials.

This case study explores how I managed production at volume within an established system, with the delivery of 300+ EOSS assets as the clearest example.

<a id="the-assignment"></a>
## The Assignment: Working Inside an Established Global Creative Environment

The assignment required me to become operational quickly within an established global creative environment. Each brief defined the assets required for an activation, while supporting materials, existing campaign assets and templates provided the foundation for production.

The work covered **EOSS, CK Jeans Spring, June Refresh, CK Pride Photo Booth and Geofencing Local Pride Moments**. My responsibility was to translate approved campaign materials into the digital formats specified in each brief.

**My focus:** understand the existing setup, work within its processes and keep production moving efficiently through review and delivery.

<a id="the-role"></a>
## The Role: Producing Within an Existing System

I started by establishing a clear understanding of the brief, its requirements and all supporting materials. Before escalating questions, I checked the available information, reviewed relevant previous assets and investigated whether the answer was already documented.

When clarification was needed, I made sure to define the issue and formulate a specific, actionable question. This helped avoid unnecessary back-and-forth and made it easier for the relevant person to respond.

![Calvin Klein exclusive metallic module — desktop layout](images/CKS_Exclusive_Metallic_Module%203_W_Desktop_Layout.jpg)

**Routing questions through the right channels:** Not every question needed to go directly to Marketing. Creative-related questions were first aligned with the relevant creative lead on the project, whether a Senior Designer, Art Director or Creative Director. If further clarification was needed, I worked with the Producer or Project Manager to define the question clearly and agree on the appropriate next step.

Questions concerning the brief's context, scope or requirements were routed through the Producer, who coordinated communication between Creative, Marketing and campaign stakeholders. Where appropriate, the Producer consolidated feedback and clarification requests before taking them to Marketing, avoiding fragmented or parallel conversations.

For straightforward operational issues, such as broken links, missing files or inaccessible assets, direct communication was appropriate.

This approach established clear communication routes, maintained ownership and accountability, and minimised unnecessary interruptions and communication overhead for the creative production team.

<a id="working-method"></a>
## From Brief to Production

Once the brief and supporting materials were understood, I translated the requirements into a clear production workflow. Before starting, I checked:

- The requested deliverables and asset specifications.
- Approved campaign assets and creative direction.
- Existing templates and live assets available for reuse.
- Relevant platform and format requirements.
- The latest project information and any changes to the brief.
- Feedback and decisions already communicated.
- Outstanding information, materials or approvals needed before production or review could proceed.

I worked within Calvin Klein's project-management environment in Wrike and used Slack conversations and other project communications to stay aligned with updates. When requirements changed, I assessed the impact on work already in progress, production time and delivery priorities.

**Creative alignment and review:** Before moving work into the wider review process, I checked that the assets were complete against the brief and relevant requirements. Where a creative lead was assigned to the project, I shared the work with them first for internal creative review. I then incorporated the feedback, updated the assets and submitted the completed work through the relevant system.

This ensured that the work was aligned internally before entering the wider review process.

## The Scale: EOSS, 300+ Assets in Two Weeks

EOSS (End of Season Sale) was the largest production assignment during the placement. Within approximately two weeks, I personally produced and exported **300+ digital assets**.

**300+ assets · ~2 weeks**

![EOSS main landing page — desktop](images/PLACEHOLDER-eoss-main-landing-desktop.jpg)

The campaign progressed through several promotional stages, including Further Reductions, Final Reductions, Last Chance and Final Hours. Each stage introduced new production requirements while maintaining consistency with the overall campaign identity.

I adapted approved campaign materials to meet the changing requirements of each stage, maintaining the established visual language across all deliverables.

![EOSS campaign progression — Main EOSS, Further Reductions, Final Reductions, Last Chance, Final Hours](images/PLACEHOLDER-eoss-campaign-progression.jpg)

**Managing production at volume:** With multiple campaign requests running in parallel, I aligned priorities with the Producer or Project Manager and kept them informed of production progress, outstanding work and dependencies.

When a project was waiting for information, feedback or materials, I made its status visible so that priorities could be adjusted accordingly. I also communicated the remaining workload and expected delivery within the day or week, giving the Producer/PM a clearer overview of production capacity and upcoming commitments.

This kept the production workload transparent and supported more informed decisions around priorities and delivery.

<a id="digital-ecosystem"></a>
## The Output: Across the Digital Ecosystem

The campaign required assets across multiple digital touchpoints. I produced and adapted creative for **web, e-commerce, email, paid social and Instagram Stories**, covering different desktop, mobile and social formats.

Each placement had its own requirements. Landing pages, email communications and Instagram Stories required different proportions, layouts and content hierarchies, even when working from the same campaign material.

My responsibility was to adapt the approved creative appropriately for each placement while maintaining visual consistency and campaign recognition across channels.

![EOSS cross-channel grid — desktop, mobile, email, Instagram feed, Instagram Story, collection ad](images/PLACEHOLDER-eoss-cross-channel-grid.jpg)

**Managing changing information and dependencies:** In a live campaign environment, requirements can change during production. I used Wrike and Slack/Teams communications to stay aligned with the creative team, Producer and my creative partner, ensuring that relevant updates were reflected in the work.

When new information came in, I assessed whether it conflicted with existing instructions, affected the production workload or changed the expected delivery time. Where a change introduced additional work, I made the impact visible to the Producer/PM so it could be assessed and prioritised appropriately.

![EOSS detail — campaign variation layouts](images/PLACEHOLDER-eoss-detail-variation.jpg)

**Quality assurance:** QA was integrated into the production process. Before submitting each asset, I checked it against the brief, current project information and applicable requirements.

Depending on the deliverable, this included:

- Required formats.
- Dimensions and specifications.
- Platform requirements.
- Content and campaign messaging.
- File naming conventions.
- Feedback and requested amendments.
- Completeness of the asset set.
- Correct destination or recipient.
- Required sign-off.

Where project information had changed during production, I also checked the relevant Wrike updates and Slack conversations to ensure that the latest instructions had been applied.

The objective was not simply to deliver finished artwork. Each asset needed to be **complete, accurate and ready for the next stage of the process**.

<a id="production-judgement"></a>
## The Production Judgement: A Template Issue

During production, I identified that some existing social-media templates no longer reflected the latest platform requirements. As these templates were already part of the established production system, I raised the issue rather than making independent changes.

I discussed the implications with the internal production and creative team, including the Production Manager, to agree on the appropriate update and assess its potential impact on the production deadline.

Once the approach was agreed, I updated the existing templates, retained the previous versions and communicated the changes during a production meeting. This ensured the team understood which versions to use during the campaign activation.

It was a relatively small adjustment, but it demonstrated an important part of production judgement: identifying an issue, assessing its impact, involving the right stakeholders and implementing a controlled change without disrupting the wider workflow.

## The Hand-off: Getting Assets Ready for the Next Stage

Following the relevant internal review and sign-off, the assets were prepared for the next stage of the campaign.

For EOSS, Calvin Klein requested master assets for delivery to its localisation agencies. My responsibility was to ensure that the supplied files were complete, accurate, aligned with the brief and had the required sign-off. The localisation work itself was handled downstream.

The hand-off followed this process:

**Brief** → **Production** → **QA** → **Creative / internal review** → **Sign-off** → **Master asset delivery** → **Downstream localisation / activation**

<a id="experience"></a>
## The Experience: Working Independently & Professional Contribution

I was able to take on production tasks independently because of my experience adapting to new creative environments, learning technical platforms and working across different production setups.

Having previously worked with brands and teams including adidas and Reebok, I was comfortable integrating into an established creative structure, understanding its processes and making day-to-day production decisions without requiring continuous guidance.

Working independently did not mean working in isolation. I understood where I could take ownership, where creative direction needed to come from the creative lead and where questions concerning campaign context, scope or priorities needed to be aligned with the Producer or Project Manager.

This distinction allowed me to maintain production ownership while respecting established responsibilities, communication routes and decision-making processes.

### What this experience demonstrates

- **Production at volume:** Personally producing and exporting 300+ EOSS assets in approximately two weeks across web, e-commerce, email, paid social and Instagram Stories.
- **Working within established systems:** Quickly learning and applying existing templates, automation methods and project-management tools.
- **Communication and escalation:** Routing questions through the appropriate channels, involving the right stakeholders and providing clear, actionable information.
- **Prioritisation and production visibility:** Keeping the Producer/PM informed of progress, capacity, dependencies and the impact of changing requirements.
- **Quality assurance:** Checking deliverables against briefs, platform specifications, feedback and sign-off requirements before hand-off.
- **Production judgement:** Identifying outdated templates, assessing implications with the relevant stakeholders and implementing controlled updates.

The experience brought together **production delivery, creative judgement, operational awareness and cross-functional communication**. It demonstrated my ability to work independently within an established creative environment, manage high-volume delivery and maintain quality and consistency throughout the production process.
